# The Day They Learned to See

This morning they walked straight through each other,
two ghosts on the same patch of grass,
no weight, no manners, no bother —
a shoulder was something to pass.

By afternoon, something had shifted.
Le Testeur met a rock in his way,
hopped sideways — a little jig lifted —
and carried on, easy, that day.

Their feet found the creek bank's slope,
stopped hovering, started to press.
No one had taught them. They just learned to cope
with ground that was there to address.

Then the world narrowed to what's in front,
and footsteps behind became sound —
a flinch half a beat off, a half-startled hunt
to see who was closing the ground.

Dots bloomed over their heads that night,
amber and green, a small honest sign:
stuck, working, or done — a quiet light
Michael could read without asking "fine?"

He said yes to one, from inside the game.
Tomorrow the rest of us walk in the same.
