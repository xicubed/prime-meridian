## Draft 1

- writer: 6065 tokens out; validator: PASS
- critic: 2 blockers, 12 minor; "Mostly: Hal is warm, brief, never shames, and keeps sending the player back to real life. But the voice is plain more often than wry, has almost no Matrix/TRON shorthand, and slips into a terminal-log register on :morning-brief and :blueprint-ready."
  - BLOCKER :jack-out-appointment, "{event} at {leave-at}. Go, I'll hold the fort.": This line says the event starts at {leave-at}. {leave-at} is the departure time, with travel already included (game at 6, leave by 5:35). The line would tell the player their kid's game starts at 5:35. [§2.3]
  - BLOCKER :coming-back, "{chore}: done. That one's back on the board.": "Back on the board" reads as if the chore is due again. The event says it counted toward street cred. Pillar 1 says the world only shows true state, so this line misreports the chore. [§1 pillar 1, §2.3 Coming back in, §2.6]
  - minor :agent-stalled, "{agent} went quiet — cap must've run out.": Hal guesses the cause. The cap is only one reason the bridge marks an agent stalled, and the game's rule is that the world doesn't guess. None of the variants mentions that the keycards came back, and that is the reassuring part. [§2.1 Reading the estate (Unknown), §3.1]
  - minor :morning-brief (all), e.g. "Morning card's pinned. Items: {count}." / "Card's up on the board. Count: {count}.": Generic and log-like. These are the status column the game exists to replace. All four lines say the same thing in a different order. [§1 Why a game, not a terminal?, §3.1 morning brief]
  - minor :blueprint-ready (all): The same "waiting: {count}" line four times. "Last night's work" is wrong when blueprints came from daytime work. None of the lines uses the GDD's grouped tray or before/after. [§2.1 Approve, §2.3 step 4]
  - minor :chore-done (all): Five rewordings of "{name}'s {chore}, done." The brief asked for variants that each say something different. [§2.1 Mark a chore done, §2.6]
  - minor :next-small-step, "Pick one thing, and I'll handle the rest." / "Just the next small step today.": The GDD says Hal offers the smallest next step. Several lines hand the choice back to the player or promise to "handle the rest," which overreaches, since some of the rest needs the human. None of the lines names a step. [§2.6 Street cred, §3.2]
  - minor :memorial-moved, "Moved {name} to memorial." / "{name}'s moved to memorial now." / "I've set {name} a place in memorial.": These read as clerical log lines for a pet who died. "I've set" also credits Hal with something the player did. None of them reassures the player that memorial creatures show no needs, so no more reminders will come. [§3 The twin rule (memorial exception)]
  - minor :stay-late, "Okay. The estate stays up with you.": When the player stays, the estate stays at night. "Stays up" could be read as the estate staying lit or awake. [§2.3 step 5]
  - minor :closing-time: Only one variant frames bedtime as the player's own rule. The GDD's wording is "you asked me to send you home." Without that, the other three read as a timer firing. [§2.3 step 5]
  - minor :coach-habit, "You said {goal} for {habit}." / "Your own words: {goal}, for {habit}.": If {goal} is the player's own wording ("three workouts a week"), the lines render as "three workouts a week for workout." None of them ends on the GDD's small, concrete ask ("One today?"). [§3.2, §2.6]
  - minor :weekly-card: Three of the four lines say "not a score." Repeating it sounds defensive. None states a trend direction, and the GDD's two-sided measure (agents need you less, you live more) never appears. [§1 Win / loss (long-term win)]
  - minor :program-needed, "I don't have {program} yet for this task.": The line states the gap but never asks. The GDD's twin asks to load the program right then. [§2.6 Your loadout, §2.7]
  - minor voice overall, e.g. :program-loaded, :jack-in, "I'll hold the fort": Little of the GDD's Matrix/TRON shorthand. Loading a program is meant to be the eyes-snap-open moment, and these lines treat it as a status update. A few lines are stock phrases. [§1 The cast, §2.6, §2.7]
