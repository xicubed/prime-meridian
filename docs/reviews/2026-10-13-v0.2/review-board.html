Round 2 conflicts the board could not settle

The Disagreement

D1Is the progression/persistence problem BLOCKING now, or MAJOR until week 6?

Systems Designer

BLOCKING. The real decision is persistent character versus disposable process, and it gets locked in at week 2 (mock agent identity) and week 3 (session-to-character mapping). After that, persistent roles become a refactor.

versus
Player Psychologist · Adversarial QA

MAJOR. Gamify v0 is week 6, outside the MVP, so no player of the course build meets the broken XP loop. Both accept that the cast problem is BLOCKING.

EscalatedDoes the persona-vs-session model have to be decided before the week 2 milestone?

D2Should XP be able to buy autonomy at all?

Systems Designer

Keep a real trust ladder. Start it below the default and fund it only with XP sources the agent can't generate itself, such as merged-without-rework judged after the merge, or XP granted by the player.

versus
Adversarial QA

On a persistent role, farmed XP builds up indefinitely, and parallel instances multiply the farming rate. Take autonomy off the XP ladder: a human sets trust per role, and XP stays cosmetic.

Player Psychologist offers a third option: move progression to the player and the estate.

EscalatedIs trust earned by the agent or set by the human?

D3Should the wow loop be pulled into the MVP?

Business Analyst

Yes. Without it the course ends with the "3D skin on an agent list" that §2 criticizes. The cheap version is a hot reload of a branch on the local client only, with "reload main" as the rollback.

versus
Feasibility Lead

Not before it's specified. Pulling the two least-specified dependencies (question and the preview path) into weeks 1–4 just makes the MVP slip. Specify first, schedule second.

Adversarial QA: only if a sandbox, a last-known-good build and a watchdog move in with it. Neither colleague's estimate includes that work.

EscalatedDoes the MVP ship without the differentiator, or take on the safety work needed to include it?

D4Where should pressure and scarcity live, and how loud can "overdue" get?

Systems Designer

The loop needs scarcity. Cap concurrent worker desks and show a visible approval queue. Michael's merge bandwidth is already the real scarce resource.

versus
Player Psychologist

Desk slots are fine, but reject the token allowance. Absence must never escalate. A missed chore should be one capped, steady overdue state, not a ladder of increasingly alarming signals.

Adversarial QA: missed chores need a defined overdue/escalation state. Desk caps also deadlock while the player is away unless workers awaiting a merge or input release their desks.

EscalatedWhat is the ceiling on on-screen pressure, and do awaiting workers count against the cap?

D5Should in-character framing wrap live agent output?

Narrative Critic

Yes. Write a character bible and a rule for how in-character framing wraps live coding-agent output. Otherwise players mostly hear "whatever a coding agent's default register happens to be."

versus
Business Analyst

No runtime Writer in v1. Use a one-page Hal voice sheet and one human-written persona line per agent in the system prompt. A Writer model rewriting frontier output adds latency and the risk of softening real errors.

EscalatedIs a runtime voice layer in scope for v1?

Drill-down · Round 1 findings and Round 2 cross-examination

Full Board

Systems Designer

Lens: whether the loop, progression, pacing, and economy actually interlock, or just coexist on the page.

Round 1 findings
Blocking

1XP and levels go to agents that don't live long enough to level up, and the trust unlock grants what is already the default.

Workers "exist while their task exists" (§7, §13). The level-3 unlock, "ask before merging", is §8's default for every agent. Needed: decide what carries XP between tasks, and start the trust ladder below the default.

Major

2The loop has no resource the player has to allocate, so there are no real decisions.

Tokens are hidden, possession is free, worker count is uncapped, and chores have no in-game source or sink. The §10c math (~$6.80) holds, but it excludes the bloated-session case (~$10 for one task) and never estimates flat-rate usage caps.

Major

3The loop pays out minutes to hours after the input, and nothing fills the gap.

~40 model calls, the QA gate, then a human merge. §10b handles latency at the frame level, not the loop level. The doc never says whether the session is a check-in or a sit-down.

Major

4Ambient tending contradicts agent lifetime and blurs the "real" result signal.

An agent nobody has asked anything doesn't exist under §7/§13. Procedural growth puts fake change into the same channel as real work. Needed: a visual signature for real-work results that ambient growth never uses.

Minor

5There is no progression arc or end state, and the progression layer is outside the MVP.

There is no early, mid or late game, and no failure state. Gamify v0 is week 6, and "MVP = weeks 1–4."

Round 2 cross-examination
  • Conflictvs Psychologist #3: no penalty on top of no scarcity flattens the loop. Move pressure to the agent side with a hard cap on concurrent worker desks.
  • Conflictvs Psychologist #4 (MAJOR): holds BLOCKING. The persistence decision is locked in at weeks 2–3, before progression ships.
  • Conflictvs Business #2: withdraws pulling an XP hook into weeks 4–5. The case for moving "!" questions earlier is stronger.
  • ConnectionFeasibility F5 + QA #1: Michael's merge approval is already the real scarce resource, just undesigned. Show the approval queue as the PR shelf.
  • ConnectionQA #3: a working trust ladder needs XP sources the agent can't farm. Drop "question answered quickly."
  • ConnectionQA #4: chores can't complete a single transaction, so they aren't a resource at all.
  • ConnectionNarrative #3 + Psychologist #5: possession has no mechanical reason to exist.
  • Revision#1 stays BLOCKING with revised needs. #2 and #3 are strengthened. #4's scope is extended to runaway growth: an unbounded source on a bounded board. #5's XP-hook request is withdrawn in favour of putting the desk cap and approval queue in the MVP.

Narrative Critic

Lens: story logic, character, tone, theme.

Round 1 findings
Blocking

1The agents "living there" are mostly temporary, so the estate has no persistent cast to grow attached to.

A worker that disappears when its PR merges can't be the character who reaches level 3. No "gardener" exists to be possessed: §7 lists only Hal, Workers, and "later" Specialists.

Major

2Pillar 1 ("Real, not simulated") is broken by the document's own ambient and specialist design.

Ambient tending is procedural. Bubble chatter comes from open-weight models. The Keeper and Clerk are scheduled jobs, and Flora's voice is an authored line.

Major

3The Oracle fantasy contradicts the possession and do-it-yourself mechanics, and the Oracle's kitchen isn't in the world.

While possessing, there is a human behind the agent. The reverse loop casts the player as a servant answering a summons. The kitchen is missing from the §6 table and from every §12 milestone.

Major

4"Conversation is the controller," but nothing defines who the characters are or how they talk.

Identity is "color, hat." The Writer is the lowest tier and owns only labels. Live replies stream straight from frontier coding sessions.

Minor→Major

5The epigraph's theme is stated but never dramatized.

No moment, arc or milestone makes the player feel the reality question. Upgraded in Round 2: neither direction of the reality boundary can be delivered in the MVP.

Round 2 cross-examination
  • Conflictvs Business F3: the crew is the only persistent named cast. Keep it as personas and staff it late. The persona is cheap; the orchestration is what costs money.
  • Conflictvs Business F1/F4: mostly concedes narrowing to Michael's estate. The epigraph must then be rewritten or reinterpreted.
  • Conflictvs Feasibility F3 / Psychologist F2 / QA F1: withdraws "the first river change you watched happen" as a signature MVP moment.
  • ConnectionNeither signature moment can be delivered. The river is stretch work and merge-gated, and "mark it done" has no write message.
  • ConnectionFeasibility F1: a heuristic "!" is an inference shown to the player as fact, which is a sharper Pillar 1 violation.
  • ConnectionThe honesty contract breaks both ways: XP buys autonomy (QA F3), and fake growth is mistaken for real output (Systems F4).
  • ConnectionThe Oracle's summons is the guilt engine. Make the kitchen the place where petitioners queue. Hal's first line is the onboarding.
  • RevisionF1 stays BLOCKING, strengthened. F2 is narrowed: the Flora-voice and Keeper/Clerk bullets are withdrawn, and the heuristic question is added. F5 goes MINOR → MAJOR, with "mark Flora fed" as the MVP's signature moment.

Player Psychologist

Lens: what a real player experiences in the first session and the first week.

Round 1 findings
Blocking

1There is no first hour. A new player starts with an empty estate and a config file.

"An empty estate is still a valid estate" (§9a). Mirrors are hand-written EDN maps, and there's no first-run flow, tutorial or demo data. Made conditional in Round 2: it drops to MINOR if the audience is narrowed to Michael.

Major

2The pitch promises instant payoff, but the pipeline delivers it in minutes to days.

"You can walk away" is not engagement design. It is the moment the player closes the tab.

Major

3A "cozy" game that turns your real obligations into visible guilt.

Overflowing inboxes, hungry pets and agents walking toward you, with no rules for decay, snoozing or throttling. Cozy games work because nothing bad happens when you are absent.

Major

4Progression rewards the wrong entity, attaches to characters that get deleted, and its one real unlock is a no-op.

"Question answered quickly" grants agent XP for the player's response speed, which is a quiet penalty for being away.

Minor→Major

5The at-a-glance status language is not distinguishable at a glance.

Idle and Blocked are both seated, told apart only by a red "!". Done looks identical to Idle. Relying on red alone is a colour-vision issue. Talk and Possess are also hard to tell apart.

Round 2 cross-examination
  • Conflictvs Systems F2: rejects a token allowance as meter anxiety. Desk slots limit what the player queues, not how fast they have to respond.
  • Conflictvs Systems F5: the doc has an emotional failure state even without a mechanical one. It needs a pressure ceiling.
  • Conflictvs QA F4: overdue should be one capped, steady state, never louder the longer you stay away.
  • Conflictvs Systems/QA/Narrative: keeps XP at MAJOR (week 6). Narrative's persistent-cast framing is the BLOCKING root cause.
  • ConnectionFeasibility F1: misreported status is worse than unreadable status. It teaches players to stop trusting the signal.
  • ConnectionQA F3: the reward system pays agents to interrupt you.
  • ConnectionNarrative F2 + Systems F4 + F5: one small visual grammar for real versus estate life and for status.
  • ConnectionBusiness F5 + Feasibility F4: visible guilt on a projector becomes visible embarrassment. It needs a presentation mode.
  • RevisionF5 goes MINOR → MAJOR. F1 stays BLOCKING while Pillar 2 stands. F4 is narrowed. F2 and F3 are strengthened.

Feasibility Lead

Lens: scope, timeline and buildability against late-2026 technology (three.js r186 / WebGPURenderer, Hermes sessions, flat-rate plans).

Round 1 findings
Blocking

F1The bridge's agent-event contract is the critical path, and the document never specifies it.

A waiting CLI session "usually just stops producing output." Possess and streaming need session multiplexing, not a websocket relay. The §13 risk list doesn't mention it.

Major

F2The rendering architecture is unstated: an immutable CLJS state map versus three.js's mutable scene graph, on a renderer agents may not be able to run headless.

The reconciler is the real engine, and nothing schedules it. Headless browsers fall back from WebGPU to WebGL2, so agents verify a different render path from the one the player sees.

Major

F3The showcase loop is out of MVP scope, and the pipeline as written can't deliver the promised "seconds" turnaround.

The river is week 7+. The realistic loop is minutes to hours, gated on the human twice: once for the screenshot and once for the merge.

Major→Blocking

F4The integration surface contradicts the document's own scope cap, and deployment exposes private data with no security model.

Six or seven integrations against a "four live data sources max" cap. A public client reaches a bridge on a personal machine with no authentication. Upgraded to BLOCKING for any deployment beyond localhost.

Major

F5Large infrastructure commitments have no milestones: the 13-agent dev crew and the generic mirror engine, with one human merging everything.

Either the MVP slips past week 4, or week 3 hardcodes Flora and Pillar 2 quietly becomes debt.

Round 2 cross-examination
  • Conflictvs Business F2: don't pull "!" and the river into the MVP unspecified. Run a week-2 spike on an explicit ask_oracle tool call and gate week 3 on it.
  • Conflictvs QA F1: keeps it MAJOR, since the river is stretch. Concedes BLOCKING if the river moves into the MVP.
  • Conflictvs Business F3: crew size isn't the bottleneck, the one human is. The plan needs a merge budget or a delegated merge rule.
  • Conflictvs "make workers persistent": persistent sessions break the cost model. Use a persistent persona record re-bound to a fresh session instead.
  • ConnectionThe protocol is incomplete in both directions (+ QA F4). Shared mirror scripts plus an unauthenticated client add up to remote code execution on the credential host.
  • ConnectionUngated hot reload can't be verified by the agents who ship it (WebGL2 vs WebGPU). "Awaiting approval" is cheap to get from GitHub.
  • ConnectionFlat-rate usage caps also cap the crew.
  • RevisionF1 strengthened. F3 gains a rollback requirement. F4 goes to BLOCKING beyond localhost. F5 refined. F2 stays MAJOR, and no colleague engaged with it.

Adversarial QA

Lens: contradictions between sections, unhandled edge cases, exploits, and unplanned fail states.

Round 1 findings
Blocking

1The game rewrites itself live, with no gate and no rollback.

On the gated path, "seconds" become hours. On the ungated path, a broken shader goes straight into the player's session, and there's no last-known-good build.

Blocking→Major

2Workers disappear when their task ends, so XP and levels can't build up.

The doc never says whether identity carries over, or what happens to XP on respawn. Downgraded in Round 2 because Gamify v0 is week 6.

Major

3The autonomy and trust rules contradict each other.

The level-3 reward is the default. "Fully autonomous" conflicts with "merges every PR." "Progression never changes the work" is false, and XP-farming is an exploit. Possess may bypass the autonomy dial and file ownership.

Major

4Mirrors can be read but there's no way to write back, and chore edge cases are missing.

There's no "chore completed" message, and nobody owns :consumes. Missed, duplicate, zero-stock, offline and typo cases are unhandled. The security sub-point (script :source) became a standalone BLOCKING in Round 2.

Major

5Ambient tending needs idle agents that the cost model says won't exist.

Persistence, file ownership (pm.env.*) and runaway growth on a one-screen estate are all undefined.

Round 2 cross-examination
  • Conflictvs Psychologist F1: "Hal, I have three chickens" is an injection path. Allowlist :source, remove scripts, and confirm before loading.
  • Conflictvs Psychologist F2 / Business F2: the rollback and sandbox have to exist before the fast path.
  • Conflictvs Systems F1: XP on a persistent role makes the farming exploit permanent. A human should set trust.
  • Conflictvs Systems F2: desk caps deadlock when blocked workers hold their desks while the player is away.
  • Conflictvs Business F4: hard-coding Flora doesn't fix the missing write message.
  • ConnectionThe exploit chain: unauthenticated websocket → possess → agent writes a mirror config → arbitrary script on the credential host.
  • ConnectionThe XP rule pays agents to create pressure: progression → more questions → more crowding → player avoidance.
  • Connection"Done, then idles" can't be told apart from a stalled session. There's no gardener to possess.
  • Revision#1 stays BLOCKING, strengthened. #2 goes BLOCKING → MAJOR. #4's security note becomes a standalone BLOCKING. #5 stays MAJOR. The crowding note is a MAJOR candidate.

Business Analyst

Lens: feature creep, market positioning, and load-bearing versus decorative features.

Round 1 findings
Blocking

1The document is three products with three different audiences, and it never picks one.

A developer agent-control surface, a cozy life-mirror for "everyone," and a self-building studio showcase. The player that exists today is one person: Michael.

Major

2The MVP cut leaves out the features that make this different from a dashboard.

Weeks 1–4 are a 3D skin on an agent list. "!", possession, the river, and "mark it done" all fall in stretch or no milestone.

Major

3The 13-agent dev crew is decorative scope that works against "Keep it simple."

Several roles have no work before week 7+. Reframed in Round 2: the crew is staffed too early, all at once, and its roles are the best candidate for the persistent cast.

Major

4The scope-creep cap in §13 is contradicted elsewhere in the document.

Twilio, two board sources, and a general mirror DSL. A cap the design doesn't respect isn't a cap.

Major

5There's no path from "the author's estate" to anyone else's, and cost is waved away for the wrong audience.

No install, onboarding, privacy or positioning story. ~$7/session is ~$150–200/month for a daily player.

Round 2 cross-examination
  • Conflictvs Psychologist #1: the real first-session player is the spectator. Make it BLOCKING only if "everyone's game" stays a v1 goal; otherwise a demo/replay mode is enough.
  • Conflictvs QA #1 / Feasibility F3: keep the differentiator through a local branch-preview hot reload, with "reload main" as the rollback.
  • Conflictvs Narrative #4: one persona line per agent, and no Writer-in-the-loop for v1.
  • Conflictvs Feasibility F4 / QA #4: security is a scope decision. Bind to localhost and cut shared configs.
  • ConnectionThe crew roles are the persistent cast. The MVP crew is the MVP cast.
  • ConnectionAdding crew adds queue, not speed. Desk slots create triage and also bound spend per player.
  • ConnectionGuilt is a symptom of the audience problem. question is the hardest event, so it needs a spike.
  • RevisionF1 strengthened. F2's recommendation revised. F3 reframed. F4 corroborated. F5's cost bullet upgraded: cost isn't "soft" even for Michael.
