02 · Escalated to the designer

The Disagreement

Conflicts from Round 2 that the board could not settle. Both positions are stated, followed by the decision being escalated.

Disagreement 1Closing time: an enforced lock or an honest nudge?

Keep the lock meaningful

Business AnalystNarrative Critic

Business Analyst: Pillar 4 is the only differentiator from free agent dashboards. A bypassable lock with a one-tap override turns it into “a cosmetic setting.” Negotiating inside the fiction is fine; a free bypass is not.

Narrative Critic: the lock and jack-out are the only mechanics carrying the thesis. Overrides are allowed, “but never invisibly.”

VS

Make it an honest, overridable nudge

Player PsychologistFeasibility Lead

Player Psychologist: on a work tool, a hard lock reads as the game blocking your own work. Frame it as “you asked me to send you home” and give a cheap, explicit escape hatch.

Feasibility Lead: a hard lock can’t be built without revoking the player’s own agent access, so the override comes for free.

Also on the table: Systems Designer would make “jacked out by bedtime” a scoring condition only, with no lock. Adversarial QA says the current lock annoys players who comply and stops no one else, so the document has to choose first.

Escalated decisionIs Prime Meridian primarily a productivity tool or a “sends you back to your life” wellness game?

Disagreement 2Should the game ever suggest raising an agent’s autonomy?

Yes: show a track record and propose

Systems DesignerPlayer Psychologist

Systems Designer: show each agent’s history and “have the twin propose raising the dial and let the player confirm,” so trust is still granted by the player but no longer guessed.

Player Psychologist: careful review needs its own visible payoff, for example celebrating a rejected blueprint that caught something real.

VS

No agent may start a trust change

Adversarial QA

Adversarial QA: a raise proposal arrives as a “!”, which makes “give me more autonomy” the most valuable thing for a forged “!” to ask. A track record can be gamed with trivial clean tasks. Show the history, but any proposal must come from game code over a channel ask_player can’t reach.

Escalated decisionWho can start a trust change, and through what channel? That first needs a definition of what each dial setting covers.

Disagreement 3What does “needing the game less” measure?

Agents doing more

Systems Designer

Systems Designer: a dialogue beat can’t fix an economy where every chapter adds “!”s. The fix has to be a measured quantity designed to fall, such as “!”s per task or minutes jacked in.

VS

The human living more

Narrative Critic

Narrative Critic: a falling “!” rate rewards agents absorbing more of the player’s life. A player who hands everything to fully autonomous agents scores perfectly while becoming more dependent. It needs a human-side counterpart.

Also on the table: Business Analyst argues that a success metric of declining use conflicts with any engagement-priced business model, so the thesis has to be written into the business model too.

Escalated decisionDefine the long-term win as an observable measure (agent throughput, human time away, or both) together with a business model that won’t quietly cut it.

Disagreement 4Write the Construct’s original script now, or defer the Construct?

Write it now

Narrative CriticBusiness Analyst

Narrative Critic: without the film lines, the Construct and Signature Moment #2 have no script. Unscoped redesign of a signature moment is major.

Business Analyst: draft the original-IP versions “now, before they are built.”

VS

Defer it

Feasibility Lead

Feasibility Lead: the Construct isn’t in any week of the schedule. Writing dialogue for an unscheduled feature spends effort before onboarding is buildable, so cut or defer it and scope the script once it’s scheduled.

Escalated decisionIs the Construct v1 onboarding? If it is, the script becomes a scheduled dependency. If not, it is deferred along with “I know kung fu.”

03 · Drill-down

Full Board

Each reviewer’s Round 1 findings with their Round 2 severity changes, followed by their cross-examination.

Systems Designer

Core loop, progression, pacing, difficulty, resource and economy math. “The moment-to-moment loop (observe, ‘!’, answer, blueprint, approve) is well specified and holds together. Above that, things get thin.”

After Round 21 Blocking4 Major

Round 1 findings

Major

F1“The day’s real decision” has no numbers, no visible stakes, and a failure case nobody handles

There is no desk count and no stated difference between frontier and local work, so the obvious play is to send everything to the free crew. The real budget, usage caps, never appears in the game. A night worker that hits a question after the door locks has nowhere to send it.

Round 2Kept major, strengthened. The opening scene shows a worker “stuck at 3 a.m.” as atmosphere, not as a rule. Either cut night-shift allocation from v1 or model it with numbers.

Major

F2Chapter wins are a 7-day streak, which breaks the “No streaks” rule

“Runs a full week with you doing only the human parts” is a streak by definition. If a missed day resets it, that is streak pressure. If it doesn’t, the chapter win means nothing.

Round 2Kept major, strengthened. Player Psychologist found the hidden streak independently, and a “!” raised at 23:59 can break a chapter from outside the player’s control.

Major

F3Progression is front-loaded and runs on two separate tracks that never connect

Street cred and loadout both make “new buildings appear.” Mail loads in the Construct even though it is the last chapter. Nothing unlocks between minute 5 and chapter 5, and street cred has no number, so it can’t be tuned.

Round 2Kept major, one claim revised. The loadout can’t be maxed in hour one, because the Construct uses sample connectors. The real loadout is paced by outside setup friction, not by play.

Major→Blocking

F4The long-term win has no mechanical hook, and the autonomy dial (its only lever) gets no feedback

Nothing tells the player when raising trust is justified. “Win the day” is won trivially with every dial at full autonomy.

Round 2Upgraded. Both win conditions reward the strategy that disables the safety model, and the stated long-term goal has no measurable quantity.

Minor→Major

F5The worm stock runs down with no refill loop

No restock chore, purchase event or receipt-to-stock rule exists. The showcase resource only counts down.

Round 2Upgraded to major, not blocking. QA’s double-count and spoofing points make the only fully specified resource unreliable in the MVP’s signature moment, but the fixes are cheap and local.

Round 2 cross-examination: conflicts

  • vs. Narrative Critic F1: Systems Designer agrees with the diagnosis but not the remedy. The reversal is in the economy, since each chapter adds “!”s, so the fix has to be a measured quantity designed to fall, not a dialogue beat.
  • vs. Player Psychologist F4 and Adversarial QA F3: make closing time a scoring condition, not a lock. A snooze with no cost would quietly delete one of the three win clauses.
  • vs. Adversarial QA F1: Systems Designer accepts the defects but not the blocking rating. A dedupe window, a restock rule and a sender allowlist are a quick win.
  • vs. Player Psychologist F5: overgrowth is the only negative feedback in the economy. Cap it and make recovery fast; don’t remove it.

Round 2 cross-examination: connections

  • Max autonomy is the dominant strategy, and it runs against the guardrails (with Adversarial QA F4).
  • “Win the day” produces nothing: no building, no cred, no chapter credit.
  • The MVP has no progression and no strategic decision, so pacing can’t be playtested before week 6 (with Feasibility Lead F1 and Business Analyst F1).
  • Loadout progression is the onboarding cliff, paced by OAuth, carrier registration and Full Disk Access (with Player Psychologist F1).
  • Waiting has no meta layer to absorb it (with Player Psychologist F2).

Narrative Critic

Story logic, character motivation, tone, theme and dialogue intent. Does the narrative pay off the promises it sets up?

After Round 22 Blocking2 Major1 Minor

Round 1 findings

Blocking

F1The central thesis (“the world sends you back to your life”) is stated but structurally reversed

Each chapter brings more of life online, you “play everywhere your agent lives,” and street cred and loadout both reward more connection. The Construct’s “I can only show you the door” leads into the estate, not out of it.

Round 2Stays blocking, evidence corrected. The bedtime lock and jack-out do carry the thesis, but they are stretch, bypassable, and limit session length rather than dependency.

Major

F2The “fixed” cast contradiction isn’t fixed: who is the protagonist, and who is the Oracle for anyone who isn’t Michael?

The player is “Neo” while the twin is “the Hero.” The twin is your body, Morpheus and the protagonist all at once. The Oracle, “the author, who approves every change,” only makes sense for Michael.

Round 2Stays major, strengthened by Business Analyst F3 and Feasibility Lead F5: the cast was written from the author’s chair.

Major→Blocking

F3Real stakes plus a “no shame” tone, with no defined in-world response to real failure or loss

A real animal going hungry is treated the same as overgrown grass. There is no transition for a real twinned creature dying, so its twin would keep saying “I’m hungry.”

Round 2Upgraded. QA reached the dead-pet case independently, Flora’s worm count is self-reported, and the snap ships in the MVP.

Major

F4The emotional peaks are written in film quotes the document says won’t ship, and the borrowed metaphor is inverted without comment

Signature Moment #2 and the whole first hour are quoted Matrix dialogue with no original replacement. In the film, machines use humans; here the human is “its hands in the physical one.”

Round 2Stays major. The IP half is strengthened by Business Analyst F5 and the inverted-metaphor half by Adversarial QA F2. The fix is writing work, not structural rework.

MinorPartly withdrawn

F5The supporting cast are job titles, not characters; the Architect gets a room and no scene

No persona has a trait, a want or a sample line. The Architect gets the most vivid setup in the document and never appears in play.

Round 2Withdrew the ask for more Flora dialogue, because a single honest status line is correct under the twin rule. Softened the Architect point to “cut him for v1.”

Round 2 cross-examination: conflicts

  • vs. Player Psychologist F4: let the player override, “but never invisibly.” The thesis needs the player to feel the door, even if they walk through it.
  • vs. Player Psychologist F5: a twin that quietly tidies up while a real axolotl went unfed is the world lying. Separate cosmetic estate neglect from neglect of a real creature.
  • vs. Business Analyst F5: keeps the IP finding at major, because the Construct’s emotional arc is made of the quotes.
  • vs. Systems Designer F4: a falling “!” rate measures agents doing more. Without a human-side counterpart, the endgame makes the inversion official.

Round 2 cross-examination: connections

  • The MVP contains none of the thesis (with Feasibility Lead F1 and Business Analyst F1).
  • The red-pill moment, the player’s first real connection, is unwritten (with Player Psychologist F1).
  • A forged “!” reopens the “what is real” theme the document thought it had settled (with Adversarial QA F2).
  • The things the story ranks highest (household, kids, a creature’s welfare) are the things the system can’t see (with Adversarial QA F1 and F5).
  • The cast was written from the author’s chair (with Feasibility Lead F5 and Business Analyst F3).
  • The Construct spoils the arc by handing over the whole loadout at once (with Systems Designer F3).

Player Psychologist

What a real player feels in the first hour, the first week, and the moment they decide whether to come back.

After Round 22 Blocking3 Major

Round 1 findings

Blocking

#1The first hour runs on the sample version, and the switch to “real” has no design

A rack of “every skill and MCP connector out there” causes choice overload, and the tutorial high is followed by a cliff into account plumbing. That is where people quit.

Round 2Stays blocking, recommendation narrowed. The first real connection should be the player’s own agent or local data, never an OAuth-gated service. Drop the full rack from the Construct.

Major

#2Waiting is the main thing the player does, and the document doesn’t fill it

The core payoff comes after the longest wait. “You can walk away” assumes there is something to walk to.

Round 2Stays major, and it is worse in the MVP: without the walking “!”, the MVP player can only watch.

Major→Blocking

#3Approval fatigue, and “spatial over textual” doesn’t work for most of what gets approved

A ghosted rock can be judged at a glance; a ghosted refactor can’t. Approvals grow with every task, and trust never grows on its own.

Round 2Upgraded. Combined with Adversarial QA F2, approval fatigue is how the safety model fails in practice.

Major

#4Forced jack-outs and the bedtime lock have no override, so the game feels paternalistic to its own audience

The estate holds their repos, mail and business. A hard lock with no user control reads as the game blocking their work.

Round 2Stays major, reframed. The lock can’t be enforced anyway (Adversarial QA F3), so it should become a nudge the player authored, not more enforcement.

Major

#5The win conditions are driven by agent behavior the player doesn’t control, and “no shame” contradicts overgrowth

“Win the day” depends on how many “!”s agents raise. A chapter is a hidden streak. Overgrowth shows a returning player exactly how long they were gone.

Round 2Stays major, strengthened. Systems Designer F2 and F4 and Adversarial QA F3 found the same defects independently.

Round 2 cross-examination: conflicts

  • vs. Adversarial QA F3: the bypass is the healthiest part of the design. Pillar 4 should be honest about being a request, not a harder lock.
  • vs. Adversarial QA F1: self-report fraud is out of scope. The double count is the real blocking issue.
  • vs. Systems Designer F3 fix: decay on absence turns a return visit into a guilt screen. Cap it quickly and front-load recovery. Show the cap gauge at the moment of allocation, not as a permanent HUD element.
  • vs. Business Analyst F2: memorial pets are one of the few sources of attachment to the place. Cut them or design them; don’t ship them half-done.

Round 2 cross-examination: connections

  • Approval fatigue plus a forged “!” means the safety gate is set up to fail (with Adversarial QA F2).
  • Every reward pressure pushes players to raise autonomy and stop reviewing (with Adversarial QA F4 and Systems Designer F4).
  • The first-real-connection cliff is steeper than first stated: Gmail and iMessage are gated for anyone who isn’t the author (with Feasibility Lead F3 and Business Analyst F3).
  • Onboarding is unscheduled and has lost its script (with Feasibility Lead F1, Business Analyst F1 and F5, and Narrative Critic F4).
  • A long wait can end in a usage cap instead of a result (with Business Analyst F4, Systems Designer F1 and Feasibility Lead F5).
  • Overgrowth after a real pet’s death is blocking for any release beyond the author (with Narrative Critic F3 and Adversarial QA F5).
  • The Director is the first casualty of approval fatigue (with Feasibility Lead F5).

Feasibility Lead

Judged against 2026 constraints: a 7-week schedule, one human Director plus an AI dev crew, a browser three.js/WebGPU client, and Claude, Hermes and local-model agents.

After Round 22 Blocking1 Major2 Minor

Round 1 findings

Blocking

F1The MVP cut line leaves out the core loop, and most headline features have no week at all

“!” is week 5 and Pillar 4 is stretch. Blueprints, keycards, the autonomy dial, night-shift orders, the Construct, the Architect’s room, the Clerk and Pepper have no week. There is no cut list.

Round 2Strengthened. “I know kung fu” is post-MVP by the document’s own definition, since it requires a real account.

Blocking

F2The bridge carries the whole design, and it’s unspecified and given one week

It has to normalize live state from Hermes, Claude Code and local models across machines without using a model. There is no event schema, no ask_player round trip and no definition of a ghosted code blueprint.

Round 2Strengthened. It now holds most of the board’s unresolved fixes.

Major→Minor

F3“Every connection comes with a sample version” doubles the integration surface, and real integrations have outside gates

Gmail restricted-scope verification, 10DLC SMS registration, and iMessage Full Disk Access against an undocumented schema.

Round 2Downgraded for the 7-week plan, which needs no real account. It stays major or worse for any real-connector release.

Major→Minor

F4The physics claims don’t fit the stated stack

Active ragdolls aren’t a helper call. Rapier runs in WASM on the CPU, so “GPU box for physics” doesn’t hold up.

Round 2Downgraded, since Week 7 is stretch. What remains is a one-line commitment to kinematic locomotion.

Major

F5The production model depends on the product, on one human’s approval bandwidth, and on plan usage caps

The crew can’t use its in-game interface until week 5. Michael approves every change from five agents. Building and playtesting share capped plans at an estimated, unmeasured ~2.5M tokens per task.

Round 2Unchanged, strengthened.

Credit given: the mock-connection test rig and the “world never waits on a model” event design are sound. In Round 2, Feasibility Lead stopped counting the test rig as a safety argument.

Round 2 cross-examination: conflicts

  • vs. Adversarial QA F1: major, not blocking. Allow one completion per chore per due window, and reword Pillar 1 to “literally what you reported.”
  • vs. Adversarial QA F2: it blocks connecting real accounts, not the 7-week plan. No real connector should ship until ask_player carries provenance.
  • vs. Business Analyst F1: “!” can’t come before talk, because the answer goes through talk. Ship talk with a static “!”. Jack-out and closing time can move into the MVP.
  • vs. Systems Designer F1: no data source exists for a live cap gauge. Declare a fixed desk count and show “desk went dark: cap reached.”
  • vs. Narrative Critic F4: original Construct lines are off the critical path until the Construct is scheduled.

Round 2 cross-examination: connections

  • Signature Moment #2 can’t happen in the MVP, by the document’s own definitions (with Business Analyst F1 and Narrative Critic F4).
  • Restocking from receipts runs through the riskiest pipe, from mail to the Clerk to game state (with Systems Designer F5 and Adversarial QA F1 and F2).
  • The bridge picks up a new requirement from nearly every colleague (with Adversarial QA, Player Psychologist and Systems Designer).
  • The Director is the first test subject for approval fatigue (with Player Psychologist F3).
  • The bedtime lock can only be soft (with Player Psychologist F4 and Adversarial QA F3).
  • Token costs hit the build schedule before they hit the customer (with Business Analyst F4 and Player Psychologist F2).

Adversarial QA

Contradictions, edge cases, exploits and unplanned fail states.

After Round 22 Blocking4 Major

Round 1 findings

Blocking

#1Marking a chore done is unverified, comes from several channels and can’t be undone, so the worm count stops being “literally true”

Texting “fed” and then clicking the canal counts twice. Clicking the canal without feeding Flora still wins the chore. Nothing restocks. Nothing says which SMS senders are accepted.

Round 2Stays blocking with a narrower core. Restock alone is minor; the blocking parts are duplicate events and unauthenticated senders.

Blocking

#2“A ‘!’ always means a real question” can be hijacked: the most trusted signal in the UI can be forged

Any agent that has read attacker-controlled content can call ask_player. The Clerk’s summary may feed Hal, and the Red Team attacks only pretend services.

Round 2Strengthened by Feasibility Lead F2 (the transport is unspecified) and by the Construct’s scripted “!”.

Major

#3Closing time is easy to bypass, leaves work stuck when it triggers, and isn’t in the MVP

Agents stay reachable in Zed and by text. Keycards, unapproved blueprints and a night worker stuck at 3 a.m. are undefined at lock time. A “!” at 23:59 makes the day unwinnable.

Round 2Withdrew “set bedtime to 04:00” as an exploit and handed the MVP-contradiction point to Feasibility Lead F1 and Business Analyst F1.

Major

#4The autonomy dial contradicts “approves every change”

Either full autonomy skips blueprint approval, or the dial controls nothing. “Win a chapter” quietly pushes autonomy up.

Round 2Scope widened. The win conditions consistently reward weakening the guardrails, which is a systems problem and a safety problem at once.

Major

#5The highest-priority jack-outs have no data source, and creatures have no way to die or be rehomed

Nothing produces “Dinner!”, and “Biscuit at the door” is a timer pretending to be real. The Keeper would keep showing “I’m hungry” over a dead axolotl.

Round 2Strengthened by Narrative Critic F3. Added that a “!” that has to walk to you can fail to arrive.

New in Round 2Major

X3An agent stalled by its usage cap has no visible state, and its keycard is never returned

An agent at its cap can’t call ask_player, so it keeps its progress glow forever and holds its keycard indefinitely. The bridge needs a “stalled” state it sets on its own, plus a keycard timeout.

Round 2 cross-examination: conflicts

  • vs. Systems Designer F4 and Player Psychologist F3: a proposal to raise autonomy is the best target for a forged “!”. No agent, including the twin, should be able to start a trust change.
  • vs. Player Psychologist F4: the lock is both too strong and too weak. The document must decide between enforcement and a nudge.
  • vs. Systems Designer F5: agrees restock alone is minor, but taking receipts in through mail opens a new attack path.

Round 2 cross-examination: connections

  • The bridge has no authentication, so “!” and “fed” can be forged locally (with Feasibility Lead F2).
  • The Construct’s scripted worker breaks the twin rule. Sample and real “!”s need to look different (with Player Psychologist F1).
  • An agent at its usage cap can’t ask for help, so it looks busy forever (with Systems Designer F1, Feasibility Lead F5 and Business Analyst F4).
  • A “!” that has to walk to you can fail to arrive (with Feasibility Lead F4).
  • A dead Flora’s chore still counts toward the daily win (with Narrative Critic F3).
