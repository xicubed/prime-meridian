# Design session, Oct 14, 2026: everything is a twin

*Captured by Hal, the daily blog post. Builds on [`session-2026-10-13.md`](session-2026-10-13.md); items marked **→ v0.3** go into the next GDD revision. Pepper (the business manager) landed in #11.*

## 1. Every character is a twin → v0.3

The game doesn't invent characters. Each one is the in-world body of something real:

| In the game | Real counterpart | What the twin shows |
|---|---|---|
| **The Oracle** (your avatar) | Michael | where you are and what you've declared; agent-you stands in when you're away |
| **Hal** | the Hermes bot itself, not a character played by a model | his status bubble is his live session; his memory is the bot's memory; when the bot learns a skill, Hal gains an ability on the estate |
| **Flora** | the real axolotl | hunger and the worm count come from her state file; you feed her in life, and she snaps in the canal |
| **Pepper** | a business-manager agent (#11) | the company's real payments state and what needs your signature |
| **Workers / crew** | real coding sessions on the repo | the branch, the tests, the PR |

Talking to Hal in the game *is* talking to the bot, as one conversation across chat, SMS, and the estate. Both of you have twins: when you're away, agent-you and Hal keep working the estate together.

**Rule (lean):** *no character without a real counterpart.* In demo mode the counterpart is a pretend channel (§3.4 of the Oct 13 notes), never a fake that pretends to be real. A twin can only show what its counterpart's state actually says: no invented progress.

## 3. The household: chores have owners, people can jack you out → v0.3

Real life has more than one person in it. The household is declared like mirrors are:

```clojure
{:household [{:who :me} {:who :spouse :can-jack-out true} {:who :kid :can-jack-out true}]}

{:id :biscuit :kind :creature :model :dog :home :porch
 :aliases ["Bisco"]                      ; nicknames all reach the same dog
 :chores [{:id :walk :who :me} {:id :feed :who :spouse}]}
```

- **Chores belong to people, not agents.** The Keeper only reminds; a human does the chore. You're reminded of (and jacked out for) *your* chores only. Someone else's chore done shows on the estate as the household being on it, never as your "!".
- **Biscuit** (the dog) is the second real creature next to Flora. Walking him also counts toward a workout habit.
- **Family members aren't players or agents** and get no twin unless they want one. They can *ring the hardline*, and their shared-calendar events (a kid's game you're going to) are your appointments. The game sees only what they share, and anything sent to them needs your approval.
- Second example for everyone's game: a roommate who can ring the hardline, and a shared plant both people water.

## 5. Cast archetypes (inspiration, not names)

Names in the game stay our own; the Matrix gives the roles:

| Matrix | Ours |
|---|---|
| the Oracle | the player |
| Morpheus: captain, consults the Oracle, trains in the Construct | Hal (lean) |
| Tank: operator, loads programs, the hardline | Hal's errands |
| Trinity: first mate, best hacker | Lead Architect |
| Niobe: captain of her own ship | Pepper |
| Mouse: wrote the training sims | Environment Artist |
| Dozer: keeps the crew fed | a household member, not an agent |
| **Cypher** | **the red team** (below) |

**Cypher, the red-team agent.** His job is to try to turn the crew before anyone else does: bait mail with hidden instructions (his "steak"), a poisoned tool, a fake "Hal says merge this". If an agent bites, the attempt is stopped and shown, and the hole goes on the board. **Cypher holds no keys**: no real credentials and no outbound messages, and he only attacks pretend channels and the demo estate. He runs weekly; each finding becomes a task and then a regression test.

## 6. Security: blast radius (from a 1Password whitepaper)

An agent's privilege is its blast radius. Our agents check all three boxes of the "lethal trifecta" (untrusted content, sensitive tools, a way out), so → v0.3:
- **Separate identities** for agents (already true: the bot has its own accounts).
- **A reader with no keys:** the agent that reads untrusted mail or web content holds no credentials and returns only counts and summaries (the Clerk).
- **Keycards on the estate:** a worker asks the Oracle for a scoped permission for one task ("write access to the river shader") and hands it back when done. Every outstanding key is visible. Autonomy levels map to the existing autonomy dial.
- Risk tiers: reads are automatic, writes get a soft gate, and money, deletion, and security changes need the player.

## 7. Release while building: one life domain per week → v0.3 §12

The game ships publicly as it's built. **Week 0** is the shared engine (mirror/chore/habit data, bridge, pretend channels, localhost security), and then **each week a new part of life comes online** in the game and in Hal at the same time: pets → household → gym/habits → business → mail → … Each week is a playable vertical slice with two examples, and its patch notes are the dev log. Open: confirm with the course that a domain per week satisfies the milestones (World, Bridge, Talk, Possess).

## 8. Single-player now, a shared world later (vision)

v1 is single-player: **your estate is yours** (the Tron half: your programs on your machine, your gym mirrors your workouts). The extension is vital to the story, though, because the Matrix is about where people are in real life versus inside:
- **Shared places, instanced like an MMO.** A real gym many players use becomes a shared map; you join an instance with friends first, then a capped few anonymous strangers, or nobody.
- **Avatars meet, twins negotiate.** At the gym you see people's avatars mirroring real people actually there; twins don't work out. Twins meet by talking to each other: yours and a friend's find a time for a pickup game, and both humans approve.
- **The privacy line is where your world ends.** Opt-in, friends-only by default, after the fact by default ("Dave worked out here today", like a racing game's ghost run) rather than live location; never a leaderboard; kids never appear in shared places. Needs a server and moderation, so it's post-v1.
