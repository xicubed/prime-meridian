## 2. Decisions

- **The cast.** The player is **Neo** (nobody wants to play a game where they aren't). The player's agent is their **digital twin** (Hal, for the author) and the one-wow agent; it carries the loadout. The **Oracle** is the author, who designs the path and approves every change; on his own machine he's both, and eventually every player becomes their own Oracle. The **Architect** is the architecture: Hermes inside the editor (Zed) driving Herdr across machines. In-game his twin sits in a room of monitors and sees only that wall; the player sees the whole world, him included, like Dorothy in Oz.
- **Hal is the Hero/Heroine, not "the Producer."** Cast members get character names, not job titles. (The instructor's point: AI doesn't know what's fun.)
- **Batteries not included.** The game is a kit car: it ships the chassis (world, rules, the crew's bodies); you bring your agent, AI logins, accounts, and life. Sample connections are the test drive, and the swap is literal: the sample inbox `me@acme.com` becomes your real Gmail.
- **Why a game, not a terminal:** you could do all of this in split panes and logs; the game gamifies the same work with a natural 3D interface, and the fun is the point.
- **Platform is everywhere your agent lives:** the browser renders the estate, but you play through your editor, phone, mail, calendar, blog, and online accounts.
- **Creatures have modes:** `:live`, `:virtual` (keep the pet, no needs), and `:memorial` (pets who have died stay on the estate). A `:personality` line in your own words ("barked at every truck") is turned once into behavior settings you approve. When a live pet dies, only you move it to memorial.
- **Limits are part of the world.** Every multi-agent setup hits caps and quotas; agents stay ahead of them and bow out gracefully (park the step, say so, hand back keycards, rest). Tonight's Round 5 ran out of usage mid-run and resumed where it stopped after an account switch: the behavior the game should show.
- **The long-term win** is needing the game less **and agents doing more**.
- Smaller: medium-poly instead of low-poly; a narrative cold open (coffee, "while you slept"); the epigraph echoes Zarathustra ("a game for all and none"); multiplayer teased (household twins meet, trade chores, negotiate the calendar, humans approve); a film-reference note (shorthand only; the shipped game uses original names and lines); the screenshot credits the scene kit.
