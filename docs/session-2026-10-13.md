## 3. New design ideas from tonight → v0.3

### 3.1 You are the Oracle *(already in this PR)*
The player has an avatar, a human among agents who have no humans behind them, closer to the Oracle than to Neo. You hold what agents can't know: intent, priorities, taste. Agents come to the **Oracle's kitchen** with questions, or find you wherever you've wandered. Possess is the deliberate exception, Neo-style "jacking in".

### 3.2 Hands in both worlds *(already in this PR)*
Agents are your hands in the digital world, and you are theirs in the physical one. You *can* fix a rock in Blender yourself but normally ask an NPC agent. Idle agents tend the estate on their own (planting trees, laying paths), mostly through procedural rules rather than model calls. **The reverse loop:** a real chore comes due, the mirror speaks in-world, you do it in real life, you mark it done, and the world updates.

### 3.3 The blueprint gate (StarCraft)
"You build the world while you fly it." Work in progress is visible, StarCraft-style: scaffolding rises, and a worker's bubble ticks through *reading → writing → tests passing*, so there is a small payoff every few seconds and a big one when a PR lands. An agent-made change first appears as a **ghost or blueprint** version; you approve it (merge) or reject it (revert). Agent changes can never take down the running game: it auto-reverts to the last working build.

### 3.4 Graceful degradation: pretend channels
Every connection has a built-in version that works with nothing set up:

| Channel | Nothing connected | Connected |
|---|---|---|
| Mail | in-game inbox (Hal, agents, and chores write to you) | Gmail |
| Phone | in-game phone in the phone booth | Twilio, iMessage, Telegram… |
| Chores | local schedule | Hermes cron |
| Agents | mock agents following scripts | Hermes / Claude Code sessions |

Same protocol for both; the pretend versions keep their data in the browser. Pretend channels only carry messages from inside the game. **Connecting a real service is an unlock**: the estate becomes real one connection at a time.

### 3.5 Flora lives in the canal
Flora moves from a tank to the river, styled after the **Xochimilco canals** (slow water, chinampas, a trajinera tied at a dock, a worm jar on the dock). The showcase river and the signature moment become one place. `:home :river` is still just data.

**The snap:** you mark her fed, a worm drops from the dock, Flora shoots up from the weeds, *snap*, a swirl of mud, the hungry bubble pops, and the jar shows one worm fewer. That gives the Character Artist one clear animation to nail.

### 3.6 Chore-done event and channels
Real-life truth must come from the player, not be assumed. Every channel sends the same event:
```clojure
{:event :chore-done :mirror :flora :chore :feed :at "…" :via :sms}  ; :game | :sms | :nfc | :voice
```
Channels, cheapest first: reply "fed" to the reminder text · click the canal in-game · an NFC sticker on the tank via an iOS Shortcut · "Hey Siri, fed Flora". *(Today's live Flora job still decrements the worm count when the reminder is sent, not when Flora is fed; changing it to decrement on "fed" is pending.)*

### 3.7 The estate learns your life *(vision, not MVP)*
Agents notice routines and build them into the world: **notice** (repeat dry-cleaner receipts) → **propose** (a blueprint: "You go here every other Tuesday. Build it?") → **build** (a building with a mirror and chore) → **function** (the grocery store keeps a pantry and drafts a cart; you approve; delivery or parking-lot pickup).

Rules:
- You approve every purchase.
- The game learns from receipts, calendar, and orders, never from location tracking.
- Every building is proposed, never sprung on you.
- With nothing connected, agents learn from what you do in-game.

### 3.8 Your digital twin
There are two of you: **real you** (the human) and **agent-you**, the digital twin that wears your avatar when you're not at the keyboard.
- **While you're away**, agent-you keeps the Oracle's kitchen. It answers the easy questions the way you would and holds the hard ones until you're back. It may handle errands too (book the haircut, draft the grocery cart), always within what you've approved.
- **When you're out in the world**, your avatar mirrors real you. When the dry-cleaner receipt lands (or you tap the NFC sticker), the avatar arrives at the in-game dry cleaner at the same moment. Two of you, one place, two worlds: the Matrix beat.
- **When you come home**, you find things already handled and wonder which of you did it. That is the epigraph, *"What is even real anymore?"*, made playable.
- Guardrails: the twin never spends money, sends anything to another human, or changes trust settings without you. It acts on your stated preferences, not guesses, and leaves a visible log ("while you were out…").

### 3.9 Moments (a new GDD section)
Signature scenes that dramatize *"What is even real anymore?"*:
1. **The snap:** you feed Flora, and she surfaces and strikes.
2. **A blueprint appears:** "You go here every other Tuesday. Build it?"
3. **Two of you at the laundry:** real you and your digital twin arrive at the dry cleaner together (§3.8).
4. **Coming home:** agent-you kept the Oracle's kitchen while you were out and already handled the easy things.

### 3.10 Street cred, not XP (the upward spiral)
XP is dropped. Progression belongs to **you**: life going well (habits kept, deadlines met, being responsible) is an **upward spiral**, and the estate shows it.
- **You decide what counts.** Habits are declared data, like mirrors: `{:habit :workout :every "3/wk"}`, `{:habit :haircut :every "6wk"}`, groceries, work or school, eating well.
- **Proof is optional.** Calendar, receipts, and Apple Health verify themselves once connected; otherwise use the honor system ("worked out" to Hal).
- **No number on screen.** Going up, the estate flourishes, new buildings appear, and agents are upbeat. Going down, it gets a bit overgrown but is **never ruined**; agents quietly pitch in, and Hal offers *the smallest next step up*.
- Cred fades slowly with no penalty, and there is a vacation mode. No judging food or bodies: "eating well" means whatever you define.

### 3.11 The crew is built the way the review kit is built
The kit's mechanism is just files, and our crew uses the same pattern:
- `.claude/agents/<role>.md` per crew member: role, lane, allowed tools, and owned files (scope enforced, not just documented).
- `CLAUDE.md` orchestration in phases (Producer → Architect → Scraper → craft → QA), like the kit's Round 5.
- **Connectors (MCP):** Blender MCP (the Environment Artist actually edits models), a browser/screenshot tool for QA (fixes "builders can't see the screen"), and GitHub (the gate and rollback).
- **Hal is the Producer on Hermes**; the crew runs in Claude Code in the repo on the class subscription.
- **The roster is data:** the game reads `.claude/agents/*.md` to decide who stands in the workshop.

### 3.12 Architecture answers
- **Public game, private data.** The game (static HTML/JS) can be public and holds no data. The **bridge runs on the player's machine** next to their own agent, the browser connects to `localhost`, and a one-time pairing code keeps other sites out.
- **Bring your own agent.** The game requires an agent, or demo mode with pretend channels. Game and bridge can ship together as a desktop app (Babashka is a single native binary); the model is bring-your-own (Claude login, API key, or a local open-weight model).
- **The message contract is one page.** It lists every message between game and bridge (names, fields, sender, effect) and is the Lead Architect's first job. Example: `{:type :agent-event :agent "w-3" :kind :question :text "Rocks: round or jagged?"}` → `{:type :answer :agent "w-3" :text "jagged"}`.
- **Scope cap.** §13 caps live sources at four, but §6 lists more. v0.3 either raises the cap or marks the extras as post-course.
- **Cost isn't "soft".** Flat-rate plans still have usage caps, and Clerk triage costs tokens, so "observe = $0" needs correcting.
- **Status needs an "unknown" state.** A wrong "!" teaches players to ignore every "!".
